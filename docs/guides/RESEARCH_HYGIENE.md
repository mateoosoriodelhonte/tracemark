# Maintain a useful research library

TraceMark preserves quotations and provenance, but the quality of a library still depends on what
you capture and how you review it.

## Capture enough context

Select the exact passage you intend to cite or revisit. Avoid fragments whose meaning depends on a
missing qualifier. Use **My note** to record why the passage matters, what claim it supports, and
what still needs verification. TraceMark stores nearby context for anchoring, but that context is
not a substitute for your own interpretation.

## Organize deliberately

Use Inbox for fast capture, then review it on a schedule. Collections work best for stable projects
or questions; tags work well for themes that cross collections. Prefer a small, consistent tag
vocabulary over near-duplicates such as `security`, `secure`, and `security-review` unless the
distinction is intentional.

Archive completed collections instead of deleting them immediately. Deleting a collection moves
its quotations to Inbox, while deleting a quotation permanently removes that local record and its
dependent saved AI results.

## Verify sources

Open the source before relying on a quotation. A successful **Mark on page** confirms an exact
current match, not that the source is authoritative or the broader context is unchanged. If the
text moved, changed, or became ambiguous, TraceMark refuses to guess; review the page and capture a
new record when appropriate.

## Protect continuity

Download JSON backups regularly, keep more than one dated copy, and run a recovery drill. Before
sharing research, create a separate Markdown or redacted copy and inspect source URLs, notes, tags,
and collection names.

Local AI output is assistance, not source evidence. Keep it distinguishable from quoted material,
verify its claims against sources, and do not apply suggested tags automatically without review.
