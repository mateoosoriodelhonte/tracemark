# Use tags and notes

Tags and notes add your interpretation to a saved quotation without changing the captured text or
source.

## Tags

Enter tags as a comma-separated list in the capture form or **Edit saved quotation** dialog.
TraceMark trims and normalizes tags, removes duplicates, and stores them with the quotation. Tags
appear as chips and as options in the library's **Tag** filter; their text also participates in
search.

Choose short, reusable concepts such as `methods`, `counterexample`, or `follow-up`. Tags are most
useful for themes that cross collections. Establish a preferred spelling and review near-duplicates
periodically. Local AI can suggest tags for selected research, but suggestions are informational and
are not applied automatically.

## Notes

Use **My note** for your own context: why the quotation matters, questions it raises, citation
details missing from the page, or the claim it may support. Notes are searchable and included in
JSON and Markdown exports. They may contain sensitive interpretation even when the quotation is
public, so review them before sharing.

Editing tags or a note updates the local record; it does not edit the source page. The exact saved
quotation remains unchanged in the edit dialog. If the selected passage itself is wrong or stale,
capture the corrected passage as a new record and remove the old one only after preserving anything
you need.

## Retrieval strategy

Search for distinctive note or tag terms when the quotation wording is hard to remember. Combine a
search with collection and tag filters, then use **Clear filters** to return to the full library.
Archived collections remain hidden unless **Include archived** is enabled.
